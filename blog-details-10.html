The Artisanal Indigo Process: Hand-Dyed Sustainable Denim

Chloe Laurent Atelier Director

Denim is a staple of modern wardrobes, but the standard industrial dyeing process is one of the most chemically intensive cycles in fashion. At Avenoir, we return to the heritage of Slow Fashion by reviving natural indigo vat-fermentation techniques that date back centuries.

Our indigo is harvested from organic *Indigofera tinctoria* plants and fermented in small, artisanal vats. Unlike synthetic indigo powder, which relies on heavy petroleum derivatives, natural indigo fermentation requires only water, lime, and organic fructose (such as dates or molasses) to activate. Each of our limited-edition denim jackets is dipped by hand multiple times, oxidizing in the air between dips to slowly build a rich, multi-layered depth of blue that cannot be replicated by machines.

"Artisanal hand-dyeing ensures that no two denim pieces are identical. The subtle highlights and deep shadow tones reflect the exact speed of oxidation and the hand of the dyer."

Sourced from sustainable cotton weaving mills in Okayama, Japan, this premium raw denim is designed to age characterfully. By rejecting mass production, the hand-dyed indigo denim represents a commitment to keeping historic craftsmanship alive while caring for the rivers and communities surrounding our ateliers.
